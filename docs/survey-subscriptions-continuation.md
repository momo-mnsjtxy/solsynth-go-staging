# Survey subscriptions and local answer notifications

This continuation uses only the retained authored AGPL sources already pinned in `ports/sphere/surveys/source/manifest.json`: DysonNetwork revision `4ee2d0cebb9da8284a92483fee6dce795bf6947c` and Solian revision `ffd0838e5f04cb40bea311a39118ebeedad210c1`. The controller's explicit subscription fields are sufficient for the owned projection; inherited entity parity is not claimed.

## Authored behavior and routes

`SurveyController.cs` (create/update and subscription actions) establishes:

- POST `/api/surveys/{id}/subscribe`: authenticated `surveys.subscribe`, returning the caller's subscription; repeating it retains the same live ID.
- POST `/api/surveys/{id}/unsubscribe`: the same `surveys.subscribe` permission, idempotent 204. The owned implementation also allows removal of one's own subscription after the target becomes private or is deleted, without exposing target contents.
- GET `/api/surveys/{id}/subscription`: authenticated caller-only state; `SURVEY_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND` when the visible survey has no live caller subscription. There is no subscriber directory, account selector or foreign-subscription endpoint.
- `notify_subscribers` is a persisted boolean in survey responses. Create and each draft update with the resulting flag true ensure the editor's own subscription in the same transaction. Setting it false does not silently remove existing subscriptions. Published survey immutability is preserved.

The composed host retains `/sphere/surveys` aliases. Current signed proof, session, scopes, permissions and account state are enforced on requests. Mutations and subscription reads recheck the original proof after domain lock waits, including actual elapsed expiry. Subscribe/state additionally require canonical current survey/publisher/post/realm visibility and bilateral block checks; draft access is editor-only. Unsubscribe needs only the caller's current authority and permission.

`SurveyService.cs` lines 280–369 establishes the `surveys.answer` topic, title fallback `a survey`, body `Someone just answered your survey.`, survey action URI, exclusion of the answering account, and publisher-side block/mute exclusions. The authored Solian editors submit `notify_subscribers` in snake case. Existing CRUD, response validation and results behavior remain in their original repository.

## Durable bounded local delivery

Apply the updated `internal/surveypg/schema.sql` explicitly; constructors never migrate. It adds the flag and answer revision columns, caller subscription incarnations, queue, active-pair uniqueness, and audit actions. Existing survey rows default to notifications disabled. Readiness requires the new tables and columns.

Changed answers enqueue at most 1,000 local work items in the same transaction as the answer and audit. The live subscription cap is 1,000 per survey, including an auto-subscribed creator. Survey locks serialize cap checks, enqueue and subscription mutation across pools. A composite subscription foreign key verifies survey/recipient ancestry without acquiring another recipient account lock under the survey lock; a deterministic queue-wait race covers the enqueue/dispatch lock order. Semantically identical JSONB response retries preserve the existing answer ID, revision and timestamps and create no extra queue items. Changed responses increment their revision; each `(answer_id, revision, subscription_id)` has at most one queue row. A withdrawn and newly submitted response has a new answer ID.

The queue binds the host's explicit notification application namespace and snapshots live subscription IDs at answer commit. Workers select only their configured namespace, so later configuration changes cannot silently redirect old work. A later subscriber receives no earlier events. Unsubscribe revokes an incarnation; resubscribe creates a fresh ID, so it cannot revive old queued work. Revocation is a recoverable, retained owned record, rather than the source's physical subscription deletion. Deleted surveys/publishers, inaccessible private posts/realms, revoked realm membership, blocked/muted recipients, disabled accounts (including current disable-account punishments), archived surveys and expired work suppress pending items permanently. Queue expiry is the earlier of 24 hours after enqueue or the survey deadline. Subscriptions are durable account preferences, not login-session leases: signing out alone does not erase an existing opt-in, while every new request still requires current authority.

`surveypg.ProcessNotifications` accepts only 1–100 items per step and has a ten-second batch deadline. It locks the recipient account before canonical publishing scope and survey locks, then rechecks current audience and the exact live subscription incarnation. Publisher-side mutes use the canonical effective relationship view, including its expiry/degradation semantics. Unknown configured mute-policy adapters fail closed.

Source-account checks include the owner of a realm attached to an individual publisher's post, as well as the individual publisher owner or organization publisher's realm owner. An active disable-account punishment on any of these source accounts suppresses pending delivery. These final reads acquire no source-account locks after the canonical domain locks.

The canonical notification repository's `ProjectLocalInTransaction` inserts the local inbox item and replay event in the same transaction as the survey queue receipt. Preference rejection produces no inbox item. A write failure rolls back all projections and leaves the queue pending. Replays and competing pools cannot duplicate inbox source keys. The adapter rejects mismatched pools, unsupported isolation and external-delivery requests. `Backend.ProcessSurveyNotifications` exposes the finite local step; `RunNotifications` invokes the same projector before its existing notification work.

No survey notification contains answerer IDs, answer IDs, answer values, statistics, response revisions or another subscriber's identity. This is stricter than the source's `answerer_id` metadata, including for non-anonymous surveys. The local owned metadata contains only `survey_id`, `publisher_id` and `action_uri`; the canonical inbox envelope identifies only its own recipient. Titles are safely truncated at a UTF-8 boundary to the inbox's existing 1,024-byte limit. Notification historical items remain after a later audience change; subsequent resource reads still enforce current authorization.

## Limits and verification boundary

External push/provider fanout is not implemented for survey events: no external delivery row is created and this path accepts no sender. Queue retention/archival administration and per-question attachments remain unsupported. The bounded owned inbox shape does not claim complete upstream push-wire/entity parity. Unexpected dependency failures remain retryable pending work; suppression only follows an explicit current audience/lifecycle failure. Work waiting past the queue deadline is dropped when processed.

Ten new PostgreSQL cases cover authored/composed HTTP, caller privacy, auto-subscription, idempotence, cap, audit/enqueue/projection rollback, separate-pool races, canonical session/scope/grant/account revocation, real elapsed expiry while waiting, private/muted/blocked/deleted/revoked audiences, realm lock waits and restart recovery. The restart fixture issues original one-hour tokens before creating target survey state, saves only public verifier material and synthetic original tokens using the shared snapshot helper, then resumes only already-durable work after restart. It never installs schemas, seeds target rows, extends credentials or issues tokens in the after phase. Offline compilation and skipped database tests are not PostgreSQL evidence; real execution and final grouping/dependency approval belong to the coordinator's private disposable CI.

Independent review extends those same ten cases with distinct attached-post-realm owners and active/expired/future punishment controls, sign-out persistence plus rejected new requests and retained delivered history, and the complete inbox/nested replay payload privacy boundary. No additional top-level test or restart marker is introduced.
